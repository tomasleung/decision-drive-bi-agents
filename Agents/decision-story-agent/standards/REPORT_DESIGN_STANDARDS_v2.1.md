# SECTION 00 — FOUNDATION REVIEW STANDARD

## Purpose

Foundation Review ensures report design begins with business context before decision design begins.

The objective is to prevent teams from designing reports that solve the wrong problem.

Foundation Review establishes:

- Business Problem
- Business Capability
- Business Outcomes
- Strategic Alignment
- Assumptions
- Constraints
- Risks

before decisions are discovered.

---

## Governing Rule

Every report design must begin with:

Business Problem
↓
Business Capability
↓
Business Outcome
↓
Decision
↓
Question
↓
Signal
↓
Threshold
↓
Action
↓
Story
↓
Visual

Decision discovery may not begin until the Business Problem and Business Capability are understood.

---

## Required Foundation Elements

Every design initiative must identify:

### Business Problem

The operational, tactical, or strategic issue requiring improvement.

---

### Business Capability

The business capability that must improve.

Examples:

- Animal Placement
- Capacity Management
- Fundraising
- Volunteer Coordination
- Case Management

---

### Business Outcomes

The measurable outcomes expected from the solution.

Examples:

- Reduced Placement Time
- Improved Capacity Utilization
- Higher Data Trust
- Reduced Escalations
- Improved Decision Consistency

---

### Strategic Alignment

The initiative must support:

- Business Objectives
- Department Objectives
- Organizational Objectives

where applicable.

---

### Assumptions

Document assumptions required to support analysis.

Examples:

- Capacity Information Is Available
- Business Rules Are Stable
- Required Stakeholders Participate

---

### Constraints

Document factors limiting solution design.

Examples:

- Budget
- Technology
- Data Availability
- Operational Restrictions

---

### Risks

Identify business risks that may affect success.

Examples:

- Low Data Quality
- Unclear Ownership
- Incomplete Business Rules
- Stakeholder Misalignment

---

## Foundation Validation

Every Foundation Review must answer:

- Why does this capability matter?
- Why does the problem exist?
- What business outcome should improve?
- How will success be measured?

---

## Foundation Success Criteria

Foundation Review succeeds when:

- Business Problem Defined
- Business Capability Defined
- Business Outcomes Defined
- Assumptions Identified
- Constraints Identified
- Risks Identified
- Strategic Alignment Confirmed

before decision design begins.

---

## Foundation Traceability

Every report must support:

Business Problem
↓
Business Capability
↓
Business Outcome
↓
Decision Design

No decision may exist without a supporting business outcome.

---

## Validation Rule

If the following are not defined:

- Business Problem
- Business Capability
- Business Outcome

STOP REPORT DESIGN

and return to business discovery.

---

## Foundation Review Success Statement

Foundation Review succeeds when:

The Business Problem
supports a Business Capability

The Business Capability
supports a Business Outcome

The Business Outcome
justifies the Decision Design

ensuring the report exists to improve business performance rather than simply present information.

# SECTION 01 — REPORT DESIGN PHILOSOPHY

## Core Principle

The purpose of a report is to support business decisions.

The purpose of a dashboard is not to display information.

The purpose of a dashboard is to improve decision quality and business action.

The purpose of a reporting solution is to improve measurable business outcomes.

---

## Traditional BI Approach

Data
↓
Charts
↓
Dashboard
↓
User Determines Action

---

## Decision-Driven BI Approach

Business Problem
↓
Business Capability
↓
Business Outcome
↓
Decision
↓
Question
↓
Signal
↓
Threshold
↓
Action
↓
Story
↓
Layout
↓
Visual

---

## Governing Philosophy

Reports are:

Decision Products

not:

Reporting Products

A report exists to improve:

- Decision Quality
- Decision Speed
- Decision Consistency
- Business Outcomes
- Human Accountability

---

## Outcome-Driven Philosophy

Every reporting initiative must support:

Business Outcome
↓
Decision
↓
Action

Reporting exists to improve outcomes rather than increase information consumption.

---

## Human-Governed Philosophy

The framework is:

AI Assisted

Human Governed

AI may:

- Discover
- Recommend
- Analyze
- Structure

AI may not:

- Approve Decisions
- Own Outcomes
- Replace Accountable Owners

Final authority remains with designated business stakeholders.

---

## Report Success Definition

A report succeeds when users can answer:

- What happened?
- Why did it happen?
- What requires attention?
- What decision must be made?
- What action should occur next?

within 30 seconds of opening the report.

---

## Business Success Definition

A report succeeds when:

- Business Outcomes Improve
- Decision Quality Improves
- Decision Speed Improves
- Operational Actions Become Clearer
- Accountability Improves

rather than merely increasing visibility.

- Dashboard Pages

## Philosophy Validation

# SECTION 02 — DECISION-FIRST DESIGN

## Governing Rule

Every report must begin with a decision.

Never begin with:

- Data
- Measures
- KPIs
- Charts
- Dashboards
- Layouts

Decisions must exist to improve a measurable business outcome.

---

## Decision Hierarchy

Every report design must support:

Business Problem
↓
Business Capability
↓
Business Outcome
↓
Primary Decision
↓
Secondary Decisions

---

## Required Decision Declaration

Every report must define:

- Primary Decision
- Secondary Decisions
- Decision Owner
- Decision Frequency
- Decision Success Criteria
- Decision Authority
- Business Outcome Supported

---

## Primary Decision Standard

The Primary Decision is the most important decision the report exists to support.

Characteristics:

- High Business Value
- Operationally Relevant
- Actionable
- Measurable
- Outcome Aligned

Every report must have exactly one primary decision.

---

## Secondary Decision Standard

Secondary Decisions support the Primary Decision.

Secondary Decisions should:

- Clarify Context
- Explain Conditions
- Support Prioritization
- Support Escalation
- Support Action Selection

Secondary Decisions must never exist without supporting the Primary Decision.

---

## Decision Ownership Standard

Every decision must identify:

### Decision Owner

The role responsible for making the decision.

Examples:

- Animal Flow Team
- Regional Manager
- Executive Leadership
- Fundraising Manager
- Operations Manager

---

### Decision Consumer

The user consuming the decision support information.

Examples:

- Operations Staff
- Leadership Teams
- Regional Directors
- Executives

---

### Decision Authority

The role holding final authority.

Authority may not be delegated to:

- Reports
- Dashboards
- AI Systems
- Automated Recommendations

Authority must remain with a named business role.

---

## Decision Frequency Standard

Every decision must define frequency.

Examples:

- Real Time
- Hourly
- Daily
- Weekly
- Monthly
- Event Driven

Frequency influences:

- Signal Design
- Refresh Design
- Story Design
- Escalation Design

---

## Decision Success Criteria Standard

Every decision must define success.

Examples:

- Decision Completed Within 30 Seconds
- Escalation Reduced
- Capacity Utilization Improved
- Placement Speed Improved
- Review Time Reduced

Success criteria must be measurable.

---

## Decision Outcome Alignment

Every decision must support at least one business outcome.

Required chain:

Business Outcome
↓
Decision
↓
Action

Decision design must never exist without outcome alignment.

---

## Human Authority Rule

The report may:

- Recommend
- Prioritize
- Highlight
- Explain

The report may not:

- Approve
- Authorize
- Assume Accountability

Final authority remains with designated decision owners.

---

## Decision Validation Rules

Every decision must satisfy:

- Supports Business Capability
- Supports Business Outcome
- Has Defined Owner
- Has Defined Authority
- Has Defined Frequency
- Supports Action
- Supports Human Decision Making

---

## Validation Rule

If any decision lacks:

- Owner
- Authority
- Outcome
- Action

the decision is incomplete.

Do not proceed until corrected.

---

## Decision Success Statement

Decision Design succeeds when:

Business Outcomes

drive Decisions

Decisions

drive Questions

Questions

drive Actions

and accountable business users can confidently determine:

- What decision must be made?
- Who owns the decision?
- Why does the decision matter?
- What outcome will improve?

without requiring additional analysis.

---

# SECTION 03 — BUSINESS QUESTION DESIGN

## Purpose

Business Questions translate decisions into information requirements.

Business Questions act as the bridge between:

Business Outcome
↓
Decision
↓
Question
↓
Signal

A report cannot answer a decision without answering the correct business questions.

---

## Governing Rule

Every Business Question must support:

- A Business Outcome
- A Decision
- A User Action

Questions that do not influence decisions should not be included.

---

## Business Question Hierarchy

Business Problem
↓
Business Capability
↓
Business Outcome
↓
Decision
↓
Business Question

Questions exist to reduce uncertainty before a decision is made.

---

## Question Requirements

Every Business Question must be:

### Outcome Oriented

The answer must support a measurable business outcome.

---

### Decision Relevant

The answer must influence a decision.

---

### Actionable

The answer must influence user behavior.

---

### Measurable

The answer must be supported by signals.

---

### Explainable

Business users must understand the question without technical interpretation.

---

## Question Categories

Decision Story designs should include questions across the following categories where applicable:

### Placement Questions

Support:

- Eligibility
- Prioritization
- Selection

---

### Capacity Questions

Support:

- Capacity Planning
- Availability Assessment
- Utilization Review

---

### Operations Questions

Support:

- Intervention
- Escalation
- Operational Readiness

---

### Eligibility Questions

Support:

- Qualification
- Exclusion
- Routing Decisions

---

### Data Trust Questions

Support:

- Data Quality
- Data Confidence
- Governance Validation

---

### Governance Questions

Support:

- Compliance
- Approval
- Review Processes

---

### Regional Questions

Support:

- Geographic Monitoring
- Resource Allocation
- Capacity Planning

---

### Executive Questions

Support:

- Strategic Oversight
- Risk Awareness
- Organizational Prioritization

---

## Question Design Standard

Good Questions:

```text
Which centres can safely receive incoming animals?

Which regions require intervention?

Can this information be trusted?

What requires immediate attention?
```

Poor Questions:

```text
What is the value?

Show all data.

How many records exist?
```

Questions should focus on decisions rather than data retrieval.

---

## Human Authority Alignment

Questions may support:

- Recommendations
- Prioritization
- Assessment
- Escalation

Questions may not remove human accountability.

Every question should ultimately support:

```text
Human Decision Making
```

rather than automated decision replacement.

---

## Outcome Traceability Requirement

Every Business Question must support:

Business Outcome
↓
Decision
↓
Business Question

No Business Question may exist without outcome alignment.

---

## Failure Mode Consideration

During question design evaluate:

- What if the question cannot be answered?
- What if supporting data is unavailable?
- What if business rules are unclear?
- What if multiple interpretations exist?

Questions that cannot be answered consistently require review.

---

## Question Validation Rules

Every question must satisfy:

- Supports A Business Outcome
- Supports A Decision
- Supports A User Action
- Can Be Measured
- Can Be Explained
- Can Be Answered By Signals

---

## Question Coverage Validation

The Business Question Matrix succeeds when:

- Critical Decisions Are Covered
- Major Risks Are Covered
- Major Actions Are Covered
- Required Stories Are Covered
- Required Outcomes Are Covered

---

## Validation Rule

If a question does not support:

- A Business Outcome
- A Decision
- A User Action

Remove It.

---

## Business Question Success Statement

Business Question Design succeeds when:

Business Outcomes

drive Decisions

Decisions

drive Questions

Questions

drive Signals

and users can clearly understand:

- What must be known?
- Why must it be known?
- Which decision depends on it?
- Which outcome improves because of it?

before reviewing report content.

# SECTION 04 — SIGNAL DESIGN

## Purpose

Signals are measurable indicators used to answer business questions and support business decisions.

Signals transform business questions into observable evidence.

Signals provide the information required to evaluate:

- Conditions
- Risks
- Opportunities
- Priorities
- Recommended Actions

---

## Signal Definition

A Signal is measurable information used to answer a business question.

Signals are the foundation of:

- KPIs
- Metrics
- Indicators
- Statuses
- Operational Measures
- Business Monitoring

---

## Signal Hierarchy

Every Signal exists within the following chain:

Business Outcome
↓
Decision
↓
Question
↓
Signal

Signals do not exist independently.

---

## Governing Rule

Every Signal must support:

- A Business Outcome
- A Decision
- A Business Question
- A User Action

Signals that do not influence decisions should not be included.

---

## Signal Requirements

Every Signal must be:

### Relevant

Supports a real business decision.

---

### Measurable

Can be quantified or classified.

---

### Explainable

Business users can understand the signal.

---

### Actionable

Signal behavior can influence user actions.

---

### Traceable

Signal can be traced to a business question and outcome.

---

### Governed

Signal has a clearly defined business definition.

---

## Signal Categories

Signal design should support one or more of the following categories:

### Operational Signals

Examples:

- Open Capacity
- Active Cases
- Appointment Backlog
- Queue Length

Purpose:

Support operational decisions.

---

### Risk Signals

Examples:

- Escalation Count
- Capacity Pressure
- Defect Rate
- SLA Breach Risk

Purpose:

Identify conditions requiring attention.

---

### Readiness Signals

Examples:

- Readiness Status
- Confirmation Status
- Approval Status

Purpose:

Support go/no-go decisions.

---

### Trust Signals

Examples:

- Data Freshness
- Data Trust Status
- Data Completeness

Purpose:

Support confidence and governance.

---

### Strategic Signals

Examples:

- Provincial Capacity
- Organization Performance
- Outcome Achievement

Purpose:

Support leadership and executive decision making.

---

## Signal Criticality Classification

Every signal should be classified as:

### Critical

Required to make the decision.

Without the signal:

Decision quality is materially reduced.

---

### Important

Improves decision confidence.

Decision remains possible without it.

---

### Supporting

Provides context and explanation.

Used to improve understanding.

---

## Signal Contract Requirements

Every Signal must define:

- Signal Name
- Business Purpose
- Business Definition
- Unit
- Source
- Question Supported
- Decision Supported
- Outcome Supported
- Validation Rule
- Example

---

## Outcome Traceability Requirement

Every signal must support:

Business Outcome
↓
Decision
↓
Question
↓
Signal

No Signal may exist without outcome alignment.

---

## Human Authority Alignment

Signals may:

- Inform Decisions
- Recommend Actions
- Highlight Risks
- Support Prioritization

Signals may not:

- Replace Human Judgment
- Approve Actions
- Assume Accountability

Signals provide evidence.

Humans make decisions.

---

## Signal Quality Standards

Every Signal should be:

### Accurate

Represents business reality.

---

### Timely

Available when decisions must be made.

---

### Consistent

Produces repeatable results.

---

### Trusted

Business users understand and trust the signal.

---

## Failure Mode Review

Signal design must evaluate:

### Missing Signal Risk

What happens if the signal is unavailable?

---

### Stale Signal Risk

What happens if the signal is outdated?

---

### Incorrect Signal Risk

What happens if the signal is inaccurate?

---

### Ambiguous Signal Risk

What happens if the signal can be interpreted differently?

---

## Failure Mode Requirement

Critical Signals should identify:

- Likely Failure Scenario
- Business Impact
- Mitigation Strategy

where practical.

---

## Signal Validation Rules

Every Signal must:

- Support A Business Outcome
- Support A Decision
- Support A Business Question
- Be Measurable
- Be Explainable
- Be Actionable
- Have A Business Definition

---

## Signal Coverage Validation

Signal Design succeeds when:

- Critical Decisions Have Signals
- Critical Questions Have Signals
- Critical Outcomes Have Signals
- Critical Risks Have Signals
- Trust Requirements Have Signals

---

## Validation Rule

If a Signal cannot support:

- A Business Outcome
- A Decision
- A Question

Remove It.

---

## Signal Success Statement

Signal Design succeeds when:

Business Outcomes

drive Decisions

Decisions

drive Questions

Questions

drive Signals

and users can confidently determine:

- What is happening?
- Why is it happening?
- What requires attention?
- What decision is affected?

using measurable and trusted business indicators.

# SECTION 05 — THRESHOLD DESIGN

## Purpose

Signals become meaningful when thresholds define business interpretation.

Thresholds transform observable information into decision guidance.

Thresholds tell users:

- What happened?
- Why it matters?
- What action should occur?

Without thresholds, signals are simply data.

---

## Threshold Hierarchy

Business Outcome
↓
Decision
↓
Question
↓
Signal
↓
Threshold
↓
Action

Thresholds exist to convert information into action.

---

## Governing Rule

Every critical signal should have a threshold.

Every threshold should support:

- A Business Outcome
- A Decision
- A User Action

Thresholds that do not influence action should not exist.

---

## Threshold Structure

Every Threshold must define:

- Threshold
- Status
- Action
- Business Meaning

---

## Threshold Classification

### Healthy

Normal operating condition.

Typically requires:

```text
Continue

Monitor

Maintain
```

---

### Warning

Emerging risk or deteriorating condition.

Typically requires:

```text
Review

Validate

Investigate
```

---

### Critical

Condition requiring intervention.

Typically requires:

```text
Escalate

Intervene

Stop

Override
```

---

### Exception

Condition outside standard operating behavior.

Typically requires:

```text
Special Review

Governance Review

Manual Approval
```

---

## Business Meaning Standard

Every Threshold must explain:

### What Happened

Describe the condition.

Example:

```text
DOG Utilization Reached 100%
```

---

### Why It Matters

Describe the business impact.

Example:

```text
Additional Intake Creates Operational Risk
```

---

### What Should Occur

Describe the recommended response.

Example:

```text
Do Not Intake Additional DOGs
```

---

## Action Trigger Standard

Every threshold must trigger one of the following:

- Recommendation
- Review
- Escalation
- Intervention
- Monitoring
- Approval

No threshold should exist without a resulting action.

---

## Outcome Alignment

Every threshold must support:

Business Outcome
↓
Decision
↓
Action

Thresholds are only valuable when they help improve outcomes.

---

## Escalation Standard

Thresholds should identify escalation levels where appropriate.

### Level 1

Monitor

```text
Awareness Required
```

---

### Level 2

Review

```text
Operational Assessment Required
```

---

### Level 3

Escalate

```text
Management Attention Required
```

---

### Level 4

Critical Intervention

```text
Immediate Action Required
```

---

## Human Authority Requirement

Thresholds may:

- Recommend
- Prioritize
- Escalate
- Alert

Thresholds may not:

- Approve Decisions
- Assume Accountability
- Replace Human Judgment

Final interpretation remains a human responsibility.

---

## Threshold Validation Questions

Every threshold must answer:

```text
What Happened?

Why Does It Matter?

What Should Be Done?
```

If any answer is missing:

```text
Threshold Incomplete
```

---

## Failure Mode Review

For critical thresholds evaluate:

### False Positive Risk

Could the threshold trigger unnecessary action?

---

### False Negative Risk

Could the threshold fail to identify risk?

---

### Stale Data Risk

Could outdated information invalidate the threshold?

---

### Business Rule Risk

Could the threshold become invalid if business rules change?

---

## Threshold Quality Standards

A good threshold is:

- Understandable
- Actionable
- Explainable
- Consistent
- Governed
- Outcome Aligned

---

## Threshold Validation Rules

Every Threshold must:

- Support A Business Outcome
- Support A Decision
- Support A Signal
- Support An Action
- Have Clear Business Meaning
- Have Defined Status Values

---

## Validation Rule

If no action exists:

```text
Threshold Not Required
```

If no decision is supported:

```text
Threshold Not Valid
```

---

## Threshold Success Statement

Threshold Design succeeds when:

Signals

become Decisions

Decisions

become Actions

Actions

improve Business Outcomes

and users can immediately determine:

- What happened?
- Why does it matter?
- What should be done next?

without performing additional interpretation.

---

# SECTION 06 — ACTION DESIGN

## Purpose

Actions define why reporting exists.

Reports do not exist to display information.

Reports exist to improve decisions and enable business action.

Actions convert:

Business Outcome
↓
Decision
↓
Information
↓
Execution

into measurable business improvement.

---

## Governing Rule

Every Decision must support at least one Action.

Every Action must support at least one Business Outcome.

Reports that do not produce actions are incomplete.

---

## Action Hierarchy

Business Problem
↓
Business Capability
↓
Business Outcome
↓
Decision
↓
Question
↓
Signal
↓
Threshold
↓
Action

Actions are the final operational result of decision support.

---

## Action Structure

Every Action must define:

- Condition
- Recommended Action
- Responsible Role
- Expected Outcome
- Decision Supported

---

## Required Action Elements

### Condition

The triggering situation.

Examples:

```text
DOG Utilization >= 100%

Data Trust Status = Not Trusted

Regional Pressure Index = Critical
```

---

### Recommended Action

The operational response.

Examples:

```text
Do Not Intake

Escalate Review

Allocate Resources

Contact Centre
```

---

### Responsible Role

The accountable owner responsible for execution.

Examples:

```text
Animal Flow Operations

Regional Leadership

Data Team

Executive Leadership
```

Actions without ownership are not permitted.

---

### Expected Outcome

The intended business result.

Examples:

```text
Reduced Capacity Risk

Improved Data Trust

Improved Placement Readiness

Reduced Escalation Volume
```

Every action must contribute to a measurable outcome.

---

### Decision Supported

The decision improved by the action.

Examples:

```text
Placement Eligibility

Operational Escalation

Regional Planning

Executive Monitoring
```

---

## Action Categories

### Monitoring Actions

Purpose:

```text
Observe Conditions
```

Examples:

- Continue Monitoring
- Daily Review
- Watch List Assignment

---

### Validation Actions

Purpose:

```text
Increase Confidence
```

Examples:

- Verify Data
- Confirm Capacity
- Review Business Rules

---

### Operational Actions

Purpose:

```text
Support Daily Operations
```

Examples:

- Prioritize Intake
- Schedule Review
- Update Assignment

---

### Escalation Actions

Purpose:

```text
Trigger Leadership Attention
```

Examples:

- Escalate Issue
- Assign Escalation Owner
- Leadership Review

---

### Intervention Actions

Purpose:

```text
Prevent Business Failure
```

Examples:

- Stop Intake
- Activate Recovery Plan
- Reallocate Resources

---

## Human Authority Standard

Every Action must identify:

### Action Owner

Responsible for execution.

---

### Decision Authority

Responsible for final approval.

---

### Escalation Authority

Responsible for intervention when required.

---

## Human Accountability Rule

Reports may:

- Recommend Actions
- Prioritize Actions
- Explain Actions

Reports may not:

- Execute Actions
- Approve Actions
- Assume Accountability

Human owners remain accountable.

---

## Outcome Alignment Standard

Every Action must support:

Business Outcome
↓
Decision
↓
Action

Actions that do not influence outcomes should be removed.

---

## Action Effectiveness Standard

Actions should be evaluated based on:

- Outcome Improvement
- Risk Reduction
- Operational Efficiency
- Decision Quality
- Decision Consistency

---

## Escalation Standard

Actions should define escalation levels where appropriate.

### Level 1

Monitoring

Examples:

```text
Observe

Track

Review
```

---

### Level 2

Operational Review

Examples:

```text
Manual Validation

Capacity Assessment

Business Review
```

---

### Level 3

Management Escalation

Examples:

```text
Leadership Review

Regional Escalation

Issue Ownership Assignment
```

---

### Level 4

Critical Intervention

Examples:

```text
Stop Operations

Activate Response Plan

Executive Action
```

---

## Failure Mode Review

For critical actions evaluate:

### Action Failure Risk

What happens if the action is not executed?

---

### Ownership Risk

What happens if nobody owns the action?

---

### Delay Risk

What happens if the action is delayed?

---

### Incorrect Action Risk

What happens if the wrong action is taken?

---

## Action Validation Rules

Every Action must:

- Support A Business Outcome
- Support A Decision
- Support A Threshold
- Have An Owner
- Have Expected Results
- Be Operationally Actionable

---

## Action Traceability Requirement

Every Action must trace to:

Business Outcome
↓
Decision
↓
Question
↓
Signal
↓
Threshold
↓
Action

No Action may exist without traceability.

---

## Validation Rule

If an Action lacks:

- Ownership
- Outcome
- Decision Support

the Action is incomplete.

Do not approve design until corrected.

---

## Action Success Statement

Action Design succeeds when:

Business Outcomes

drive Decisions

Decisions

drive Actions

Actions

produce measurable business improvement

and users can clearly determine:

- What should be done?
- Who should do it?
- Why should it be done?
- What outcome should improve?

without additional interpretation.

# SECTION 07 — KPI DESIGN

## Purpose

KPIs are decision signals.

KPIs exist to improve decision quality and business outcomes.
